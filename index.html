CONSERVATIVE

2. AI Chip Stocks Crater on Fears of Data-Center Curbs

The Nasdaq-100 fell 6.4% as investors bet Democratic officials will slow the AI buildout.

INNOVATION
OVERSIGHT
INNOVATION vs OVERSIGHT
Innovation view
  • Demand hasn't gone away. AMD's Lisa Su says demand outstrips supply and expects revenue growth above 80% in 2027.
  • Blanket moratoria would hit hardest in Texas, which holds a large share of planned US data-center capacity.
  • A patchwork of state rules and pauses slows the US in its race with China. Federal policy has aimed at a single standard.
  • The selloff looks overdone. Nvidia's forward P/E is at a five-year low while analysts see its earnings more than doubling.

Positions commonly voiced by chipmakers, AI-industry executives and pro-buildout analysts.

Oversight view
  • Voters asked for limits. Most of the 41 ballot measures on slowing data centers passed.
  • Power-hungry data centers raise electricity bills. Developers should pay their own grid costs.
  • Ohio's Gov.-elect Acton backs a conditional pause unless builders cover 100% of utility costs, a middle path rather than a ban.
  • Early safety rules and scaled-back tax breaks are overdue. Some analysts say pushback could deflate a bubble without popping it.

Positions commonly voiced by Democratic governors-elect, consumer advocates and AI-safety and utility-cost critics.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • The Nasdaq-100 fell 6.4%, wiping out $2.3 trillion in its worst session since March 2020, per Bloomberg.
  • The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell more than 12%, and Nvidia fell about 9%, per Fox Business.
  • Texas Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa pledged an immediate data-center moratorium. Ohio's Amy Acton backs a conditional one.
  • UBS estimates roughly 30% of expected 2028 US data-center capacity additions could face construction pauses.

Sourced from Bloomberg, Fortune, Forbes, Fox Business, Yahoo Finance and The National, November 5, 2026.

BULL vs BEAR

4. Novo Raises Outlook Again as Wegovy Pill Beats Forecasts

The obesity drugmaker lifted 2026 guidance for the third time even as US injectable sales slid.

GROWTH-FOCUSED
RISK-FOCUSED
GROWTH vs RISK
Growth-focused view
  • Pill sales of DKK 5.39 billion beat forecasts and grew 53% from the prior quarter.
  • Novo estimates it holds about 80% of new US pill prescriptions, so it is winning the funnel for future refills.
  • Rollout to more than 20 markets next year widens the addressable base.
  • Adjusted operating profit rose 27%, which shows the turnaround is reaching profits.

Positions commonly voiced by pharma bulls and growth investors.

Risk-focused view
  • Management still expects US sales to fall in 2026, so this is a smaller decline, not growth.
  • US injectable Wegovy fell 13%, and the pill is still a small share of expected Wegovy sales.
  • Eli Lilly remains a strong competitor, and patent expiries loom.
  • The stock has lost about two-thirds of its value since June 2024, and one good quarter doesn't rebuild that.

Positions commonly voiced by cautious analysts and risk-focused investors.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Wegovy pill sales were DKK 5.39 billion (about $810 million) against about DKK 4.62 billion expected.
  • Guidance is now -2% to +2% at constant exchange rates, raised from an earlier range allowing declines of up to 6%.
  • US injectable Wegovy sales fell 13%, though combined US Wegovy sales rose 21%.
  • Novo still expects US sales to decline in 2026 and lists slowing pill prescriptions and Lilly competition as risks.

Sourced from Reuters (via Euronext), BioPharma Dive, Finimize and Value the Markets, November 4-5, 2026.

5. MLB Pitches Delayed Salary Cap; Players Say No

Owners moved the cap to 2028 and raised its figure, but the union rejected it with a Dec. 1 lockout looming.

TAKE
COUNTER-TAKE
TAKE vs COUNTER-TAKE
Take
  • Revenue gaps between clubs are huge, and a cap and floor is the clearest fix for competitive balance.
  • The NBA, NFL and NHL all run on caps, and their players still thrive.
  • The floor would force 12 clubs to add about $636 million in payroll, money that would reach players.
  • Delaying the cap to 2028 and raising it to $258.2 million shows real flexibility.

Positions commonly voiced by owners, Commissioner Manfred and cap-friendly analysts.

Counter-take
  • A cap that caps player pay is a non-starter. The union has opposed one for decades.
  • Owners last proposed a cap in 1994, and that fight ended in a strike that cancelled the World Series.
  • The cap includes benefits, and teams wouldn't have to hit the floor every season, so the floor is weaker than advertised.
  • Players want expanded free agency, a nearly doubled minimum and more revenue sharing. Offering earlier free agency only if they take a cap is a bargaining chip.

Positions commonly voiced by the MLBPA, players and labor-side commentators.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • MLB's new cap is $258.2 million and its floor $180.2 million, up from $245.3 million and $171.2 million.
  • The league would grant free agency at five service years, down from six, for players 30 and older, but only if the union accepts the cap.
  • The labor deal expires Dec. 1, and a lockout appears imminent, per ESPN.
  • The union quickly rejected the proposal, and baseball players have said they will never agree to a cap.

Sourced from ESPN, Sports Illustrated, NBC Sports, Yahoo Sports and Forbes, November 3-5, 2026.
