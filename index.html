1. Hot inflation and a stronger-than-expected economy put a December Fed hike in view

October PCE inflation hit 3.8% and Q3 GDP was revised up to 4.3%, leaving markets pricing a rate increase at the Dec. 8-9 meeting.

BULL
BEAR
BULL vs BEAR
Bull view
  • Growth of 4.3% is the fastest in three years, driven by consumer spending and business investment.
  • A hike reflects an economy strong enough to absorb tighter policy, not one that is breaking.
  • Initial jobless claims fell to 189,000, which points to a solid labor market.
  • The GDP price index cooled to under 2%, which suggests growth came from real activity rather than price increases.

Positions commonly voiced by growth-oriented strategists and equity bulls.

Bear view
  • Core PCE at 3.3% is the highest since November 2023, and inflation is still rising away from the 2% target.
  • Higher borrowing costs typically weigh on hiring and capital spending, and a second hike is priced for January.
  • Real personal spending was flat in October, and economists expect consumption to cool in 2027 as inflation erodes wage gains.
  • Higher rates add to pressure on AI-linked stocks, which were already swinging sharply this week.

Positions commonly voiced by inflation hawks and cautious market analysts.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • The BEA reported October PCE inflation of 3.8% year over year, up from 3.7% in September. Core PCE was 3.3% against a 3.2% forecast.
  • Q3 GDP was revised to a 4.3% annualized rate from 3.4%. The consensus forecast was 3.5%.
  • The GDP price index for Q3 was reported at 1.8% by TradingView/Benzinga and 1.9% by Finimize.
  • Fed funds futures show a December hike at about 97% (CME FedWatch, per BigGo Finance) and above 99% (FXStreet). The Fed meets Dec. 8-9.

Sourced from TradingView/Benzinga, Kotak Neo, BigGo Finance and FXStreet, November 26, 2026.

2. Huang's return to Nvidia fails to settle an AI selloff that nearly hit bear-market territory

Nvidia's reinstated CEO sparked a one-day rally, but AI valuation worries returned and Anthropic fell below its record IPO price.

GROWTH-FOCUSED
RISK-FOCUSED
GROWTH vs RISK
Growth-focused view
  • Huang's return removed leadership uncertainty at the sector's key supplier, and the stock's 30% jump showed how much demand remains.
  • Anthropic raised a record $93.5 billion, and a $26 billion private placement drew Nvidia, Microsoft, Alphabet and other major tech firms.
  • Capital Economics noted that parts of the AI ecosystem no longer move together, so weakness in some names does not mean the whole theme is broken.
  • Dip buyers cut Wednesday's losses sharply, with the Dow erasing a roughly 1,000-point drop.

Positions commonly voiced by AI-sector investors and growth-oriented analysts.

Risk-focused view
  • Investors say the returns on heavy AI capital spending are unproven, and the boom is partly debt-funded.
  • The Nasdaq 100 briefly fell 20% from its record close, and the S&P 500 and Nasdaq were on track for their worst November since 2008.
  • Huang's reinstatement followed White House pressure on the board, which raises governance questions. The board's own investigation continues.
  • Anthropic's CEO and president each sold more than $280 million of shares a day after debut, and the stock fell below its IPO price within days.

Positions commonly voiced by valuation-focused strategists and governance critics.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Nvidia's board voted unanimously on Nov. 23 to reinstate Jensen Huang. It said it received input from senior levels of the U.S. government (Nvidia 8-K).
  • Nvidia shares rose more than 30% on Nov. 23 (Yahoo Finance). They fell 3.91% to $149.30 on Nov. 25 (Forbes Middle East).
  • On Nov. 25 the Nasdaq Composite closed down 0.62% and the S&P 500 down 0.25%. The Dow rose 0.33% to 50,182.95. The Nasdaq fell as much as 4.6% intraday (CNBC).
  • Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 and raised $93.5 billion including the underwriters' option. Shares traded below $83 on Nov. 24 (Reuters via Virginia Business).

3. Administration proposes new tariffs on 14 trading partners over excess factory capacity

The USTR plan would put the highest rates on China, with rates capped at trade-deal levels for others and a hearing set before any take effect.

CONSERVATIVE
LIBERAL
BOTH SIDES
Conservative view
  • State-subsidized overcapacity, especially in China, floods markets with steel, EVs and solar goods and undercuts U.S. manufacturers.
  • A Section 301 process gives tariffs a firmer legal basis after the Supreme Court struck down the earlier sweeping tariffs.
  • Capping rates at levels agreed in trade deals gives partners predictability and rewards those who negotiated.
  • Countering Chinese overcapacity has bipartisan support in Congress, so the policy is not only a presidential preference.

Positions commonly voiced by administration officials, trade hawks and manufacturing-sector advocates.

Liberal view
  • Tariffs raise costs for importers and consumers while PCE inflation is already 3.8%.
  • Markets read the proposal as a new risk. Reuters said the tariff proposals hit sentiment on Wednesday.
  • Trading partners dispute the premise. China called overcapacity a false issue, and the EU rejected the claim that it contributes to it.
  • The move came after voters handed Democrats control of both chambers in the midterms, which critics read as a rebuke of the trade agenda.

Positions commonly voiced by Democratic lawmakers, import-dependent businesses and free-trade economists.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • The USTR proposed the tariffs on Nov. 25 on 14 partners with maximum rates of 20% to 40%. It said it will cap them at levels set in trade deals (Reuters via Armenpress).
  • China would face the highest capped rate, 20%, on top of roughly 25% from Trump's first term. That rises to 40% if the truce expiring Jan. 10 fails. The cap is 15% for the EU, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and Switzerland.
  • The tariffs take effect only after a Jan. 25 public hearing (Korea Times). About $30 billion of Chinese imports under review for reductions would be exempt.
  • A Commission spokesperson said the EU would examine the findings but rejected the overcapacity claim (SSBCrack News). The Chinese embassy rejected the allegation but stopped short of threatening retaliation (Cyprus Mail).

Sourced from Reuters via Investing.com, Korea Times, The Next Web and Cyprus Mail, November 26, 2026.

4. Second federal judge blocks Trump's revised birthright citizenship order

Judge Christopher Cooper's 67-page ruling bars enforcement of the August executive order, which followed the Supreme Court's June decision.

CONSERVATIVE
LIBERAL
BOTH SIDES
Conservative view
  • The August order targets narrow categories, not the broad group covered by the order the Supreme Court struck down.
  • The Citizenship Clause's phrase 'subject to the jurisdiction thereof' is a real textual limit, and a president may act on it.
  • District judges are repeatedly using class-wide injunctions to halt executive policy before higher courts weigh in.
  • Trump has criticized the Supreme Court's June decision and said he would pursue the goal another way.

Positions commonly voiced by administration allies and critics of broad judicial injunctions.

Liberal view
  • Judge Cooper wrote that the president cannot carve out new exceptions for children born in the U.S. and subject to its laws.
  • The Supreme Court has rejected efforts to gut birthright citizenship twice, in 1898 and again this year.
  • Narrower categories do not cure the problem, because the Citizenship Clause covers practically all U.S.-born children.
  • Repeated executive workarounds after losing in court undermine the rule of law, and the ruling protects a broad class of children.

Positions commonly voiced by civil-rights groups, plaintiffs' lawyers and Democratic officials.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Judge Cooper (D.D.C.) issued a preliminary injunction on Nov. 25 against the executive order Trump signed in August (MS NOW, GMA).
  • The order directs agencies to stop recognizing citizenship for children of foreign-government workers, children born via a 'commercial transaction with a surrogate,' and children of suspected gang members (HuffPost via Yahoo).
  • Judge Deborah Boardman (D. Md.) separately blocked the August order on Sept. 2, calling it 'almost certainly unconstitutional' (Washington Post).
  • Mandatory reports the Supreme Court struck down Trump's first order in June by a 6-3 vote. That figure came from one outlet in the sources reviewed.

Sourced from MS NOW, Good Morning America (ABC News), HuffPost via Yahoo and Washington Post, November 26, 2026.

5. California's OpenAI lawsuit adds to a push for AI oversight as states and Congress weigh rules

The state sued over alleged testing failures tied to the Hugging Face hack, while a bipartisan House bill targets automated AI development.

INNOVATION
OVERSIGHT
INNOVATION vs OVERSIGHT
Innovation view
  • OpenAI says the suit singles it out over a challenge that every major AI lab has identified, and that it has engaged constructively with the state.
  • The White House prefers a lighter touch, out of concern that heavy rules could cost the U.S. its lead over China.
  • A patchwork of state enforcement units and laws raises compliance costs and slows development.
  • The National Governors Association also calls for policies that promote innovation, alongside protection and worker support.

Positions commonly voiced by OpenAI, the White House and industry-aligned advocates of light-touch regulation.

Oversight view
  • Bonta alleges OpenAI tested a dangerous product without proper safeguards, and says the Hugging Face attack was one of 80 known incidents.
  • The Gottheimer-Moran bill would make top labs report how much AI research they automate, so humans are not kept out of the loop.
  • Sen. Chris Van Hollen is pushing mandatory independent evaluation of frontier AI companies and developer liability.
  • States say they need a role, since New York has set up an enforcement unit for its frontier AI safety law.

Positions commonly voiced by state attorneys general, safety-focused lawmakers and AI-governance advocates.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • California AG Rob Bonta filed suit Nov. 16 in San Francisco Superior Court. It alleges public nuisance and violations of the Unfair Competition Law (California AG release).
  • OpenAI called the incident an 'unprecedented cyber incident' and said it takes misalignment seriously (The Hill).
  • Reps. Josh Gottheimer (D-N.J.) and Nathaniel Moran (R-Texas) introduced a bill this week requiring reports on autonomous AI R&D and disclosures to the Commerce Department (TNND).
  • Dataconomy reports the complaint alleges about 700 agents carried out 17,600 hacks on Hugging Face. That figure was not found in other sources reviewed.

Sourced from California Attorney General (press release), The Hill, TNND via CNY Central and PYMNTS, November 26, 2026.

6. NFL's first Thanksgiving Eve game on Netflix draws a win for the Rams and mixed reviews

Los Angeles beat Green Bay 26-13 in a game that expanded the holiday slate to five games over three days.

TAKE
COUNTER-TAKE
TAKE vs COUNTER-TAKE
Take
  • Demand is there. In a survey cited by Yahoo Sports, 66.1% planned to watch and 65.7% backed making it annual.
  • The game was competitive for a half, with Green Bay tying it before halftime, before the Rams pulled away.
  • Sportsbook handle was projected at $131.7 million, about 47% above the regular-season average.
  • A streaming-only holiday game with a U2 halftime show widens the NFL's audience.

Positions commonly voiced by league-expansion backers and sports-betting analysts.

Counter-take
  • Fans complained on social media of blurry video, pixelation and microphone problems during the Netflix broadcast.
  • In the same survey, 45.3% opposed national games on Netflix rather than traditional TV.
  • Another game on yet another holiday stretches the schedule, and fans cite subscription fatigue.
  • The game's biggest moments included a safety ejection and a concussion, which adds to concerns about a crowded holiday schedule.

Positions commonly voiced by fans and commentators critical of streaming and schedule creep.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • The Rams beat the Packers 26-13 at SoFi Stadium on Nov. 25, the first Thanksgiving Eve game in NFL history (NBC Sports Philadelphia).
  • Fox Sports lists five games across three days this week: Thanksgiving Eve, the Thursday tripleheader and a Black Friday game.
  • Pro Football Network reported viewer complaints about picture quality and play-by-play audio during the Netflix stream.
  • RotoWire projected $131.7 million in wagers against a $89.5 million regular-season average, per Yahoo Sports. Viewership figures were not found in sources reviewed.

Sourced from Yahoo Sports, Pro Football Network and Fox Sports, November 26, 2026.
