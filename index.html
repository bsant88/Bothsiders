1. Nvidia Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO After Trump Push, Reviving Chip Export Fight

Huang's return, which Trump says he arranged, drew bipartisan criticism and new momentum for AI chip export-control bills.

INNOVATION
OVERSIGHT
INNOVATION vs OVERSIGHT
Innovation view
  • Nvidia's board voted unanimously and says Huang's return serves shareholders, employees and partners.
  • Huang has argued that strict chip export limits cost U.S. firms sales and speed up Chinese rivals.
  • Trump reportedly worried about the wider economic effect if Nvidia's value collapsed, according to CBS News.
  • The administration says there is no evidence his return is a national security risk.

Positions commonly voiced by Nvidia's board, the White House and parts of the tech industry.

Oversight view
  • Lawmakers in both parties say Huang and Nvidia still owe Congress answers on export-control compliance.
  • Critics say a president pressing a board to reinstate a CEO blurs the line between government and industry.
  • Sen. Cramer, a Republican, warned that the national security risk falls on the military and citizens.
  • Backers of chip bills want location verification and congressional veto power over exports to adversaries, possibly through the defense bill.

Positions commonly voiced by members of both parties, including Sen. Warren, Sen. Cramer and Rep. Bacon.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Nvidia announced Nov. 23 that Huang was reinstated as CEO effective immediately and said the board had input from senior levels of the U.S. government (CNBC).
  • Trump told CNBC he convinced the board to bring Huang back. Semafor reported the board vote was unanimous, citing one person familiar with the matter.
  • CNN reported Nvidia shares ended Nov. 23 more than 30% higher. CNBC's headline put the gain at 31%.
  • The Hill reported Huang is due for a closed-door interview with three House panels on Dec. 3. Semafor, confirming with a DOJ official, said DOJ is probing Nvidia customers Megaspeed and Supermicro over export controls.

2. U.S. Proposes New Section 301 Tariffs on 14 Economies; Partners Push Back

The proposed duties target alleged manufacturing overcapacity and would not take effect for months, after a public comment period.

CONSERVATIVE
LIBERAL
BOTH SIDES
Conservative view
  • Subsidy-driven overcapacity abroad floods markets and undercuts U.S. manufacturers, and a trade probe is the right tool.
  • Section 301 rests on a formal investigation and a comment process, a firmer legal footing after the court loss on emergency tariffs.
  • The 20% rate on China matches an agreement already reached with the White House, so it is not a new escalation.
  • Rates are capped at 15% for partners with existing deals, which leaves room to negotiate.

Positions commonly voiced by the Trump administration, USTR Jamieson Greer and Republican allies.

Liberal view
  • Tariffs act as a tax on U.S. consumers and firms, and inflation is already running at 3.8%.
  • Democrats argue Trump has not learned from earlier tariff rounds that raised costs.
  • Targeting close allies such as Japan, South Korea and the EU risks breaking trade deals and inviting retaliation.
  • The administration is reaching for a new legal route to rebuild tariffs the courts rejected.

Positions commonly voiced by Democratic lawmakers and trade-policy critics.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • USTR completed an eight-month Section 301 probe of 16 partners and proposed duties on 14 (RTE, EUalive). Proposed rates are 20% for China and Vietnam and 17% for India, with a 15% cap for deal partners such as the EU (Plataforma Media).
  • Greer told CNBC the tariffs likely will not take effect for months. Plataforma Media reported implementation in January at the earliest, after public comment.
  • The EU says it rejects the premise that Europe has structural overcapacity, and trade commissioner Sefcovic said Washington must honor the 15% cap. China's Commerce Ministry also rejected the basis for the tariffs (NBC News).
  • Reuters reported U.S. inflation at 3.8%, about a point higher than in each of the last two years.

Sourced from CNBC, NBC News, RTE and Plataforma Media, November 27, 2026.

4. Black Friday Opens With Record Online Forecasts and Signs of Shopper Strain

Forecasters expect strong holiday sales, but inflation at 3.8% accounts for much of the projected gain.

GROWTH-FOCUSED
RISK-FOCUSED
GROWTH vs RISK
Growth-focused view
  • Adobe forecasts $12.9 billion in online Black Friday sales, up 9.2%, with Cyber Monday at $15.1 billion.
  • Deloitte found 87% of shoppers plan to take part, the highest in a decade, so demand is intact.
  • Value-hunting shoppers favor retailers with low prices, and some firms are pricing thousands of items at $5 or under.
  • Trading cards are a growth driver for Best Buy and Target, showing demand in some categories.

Positions commonly voiced by retail forecasters and industry groups such as Adobe, Deloitte and the NRF.

Risk-focused view
  • Most of the 4% to 6% forecast growth comes from inflation, not volume.
  • Visa expects inflation-adjusted holiday spending to grow just 1.1%, below last year's 1.3%.
  • Kohl's CEO described an 'increasingly choiceful' consumer, and 82% of shoppers are managing holiday costs.
  • Toy prices are up 13.2% this year, and Mattel cut its annual forecast.

Positions commonly voiced by retail analysts and some retailers citing consumer strain.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Adobe forecasts $12.9 billion in U.S. online Black Friday sales, up 9.2% (NBC News). The NRF sees November-December sales up 4.2% to just above $1 trillion (Reuters).
  • Deloitte's October survey found 87% of 1,200 consumers plan to shop Thanksgiving through Cyber Monday, the highest since 2016 (CNBC, Reuters).
  • The CNBC/NRF Retail Monitor showed October retail sales growing at the slowest pace in nearly a year (CNBC). Visa projects holiday prices up 3.4% and real spending growth of 1.1% (Center Square).
  • Planned average spend differs by source: Deloitte reports $624, similar to last year (CNBC), while Finder reports $488, down from $594 (Finder).

Sourced from Reuters via WHBL, CNBC, Just the News (Center Square) and Finder, November 27, 2026.

TAKE
COUNTER-TAKE
TAKE vs COUNTER-TAKE
Take
  • The Bears are real contenders: they overcame an 11-point fourth-quarter deficit and left Detroit first in the NFC North at 9-2.
  • The Eagles' 21-point comeback shows a resilient team that can still make a run despite the slow start.
  • Buffalo at 10-1 is the AFC's team to beat after beating the Chiefs.
  • The Rams' fifth straight win, led by Blake Corum's 184 rushing yards, makes them a playoff threat.

Positions commonly voiced by NFL analysts and fans praising the winners.

Counter-take
  • The Bears trailed for 53 minutes and 31 seconds and allowed 35 points, so close wins can hide defensive problems.
  • The Eagles are 4-7 and needed a 60-yard field goal to beat a team they trailed 31-10, which is no sign of a turnaround.
  • Buffalo's win over Kansas City was described as a slog and does not prove dominance.
  • Late-score finishes are partly luck and tend to regress, so records may overstate quality.

Positions commonly voiced by skeptical analysts and fans.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
