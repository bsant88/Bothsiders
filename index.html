1. Stocks Enter Correction After AI-Led Sell-Off as Fed Hike Looms

The S&P 500 and Dow closed Friday in correction territory, and investors are split on whether the drop is a reset or a top.

BULL
BEAR
BULL vs BEAR
Bull view
  • The S&P 500's relative strength index fell below 30 Friday, a level that signals oversold conditions and often precedes a bounce.
  • Wharton's Jeremy Siegel calls the drop a reset of expectations after three years of double-digit annual stock returns.
  • November and December are historically among the best months for the S&P 500, and a cooler inflation reading could ease fears of a half-point hike.
  • Nvidia beat revenue estimates and guided higher. Its forward earnings multiple is about 8.2, below Microsoft, Alphabet and Broadcom, per 24/7 Wall St.

Positions commonly voiced by dip-buying strategists and academics quoted by CNBC, such as Jeremy Siegel.

Bear view
  • The Fed is raising rates into a sell-off. Minutes show nearly all officials expect a December hike, and a 'vast majority' fear inflation could persist.
  • Fed officials such as Musalem see 'several' more hikes before spring 2027, which raises discount rates on richly valued AI stocks.
  • A record Anthropic IPO and a strong Nvidia report failed to stop the slide. Some investors read both as signs of a market top.
  • Mortgage rates above 7% and rising borrowing costs are weighing on housing and household spending.

Positions commonly voiced by rate-hawk Fed officials and cautious strategists quoted by AP, The Hill and TipRanks.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • The S&P 500 fell 3.7% Friday to 6,910.39. For the week it fell 8.6% per Yahoo Finance, and 8.4% per Investing.com. The Dow fell 8% and the Nasdaq about 10%, per Yahoo Finance and CNBC.
  • The S&P 500 had seven straight losing sessions, its longest streak since February 2020, per Yahoo Finance. Anthropic closed the week at $88.91 against an $83 IPO price, per CNBC.
  • Fed minutes from the Oct. 27-28 meeting show nearly all officials expect a quarter-point hike in December. Two floated a half-point, per AP. Governor Waller said core inflation ran about 3.5% annualized over three months.
  • The Fed meets Dec. 8-9, per TipRanks. CME FedWatch put the odds of a December hike near 95%, per AP via Yahoo Finance. New York Fed's Williams has pushed back on a half-point move.

Sourced from CNBC, Yahoo Finance and Investing.com, November 22, 2026.

2. UN Talks on Autonomous Weapons End Without Binding Rules

Geneva negotiations extended work to 2028 but stopped short of the treaty commitment that about 100 countries wanted.

INNOVATION
OVERSIGHT
INNOVATION vs OVERSIGHT
Innovation view
  • Existing international humanitarian law already applies to all weapons, including autonomous systems, per the UN's own guidance.
  • Rushing a binding treaty could freeze definitions before the technology is understood. Not every autonomous weapon uses AI.
  • Militaries say autonomy can be used responsibly under human command and control. Canada's Defence Department takes this position.
  • Major military powers see strategic risk in binding limits, since 'no country wants to come second' in the race to develop new weapons, per NPR.

Positions commonly voiced by defense ministries and governments resisting binding limits, as reported by CBC and NPR.

Oversight view
  • UN Secretary-General Guterres sought a legally binding instrument with clear prohibitions and restrictions.
  • Humanitarian groups warn that military AI is advancing far faster than diplomacy.
  • Campaigners want bans on systems that target people and on weapons that cannot stay under meaningful human control.
  • Under the compromise, weapons that can make life-and-death decisions stay unregulated for at least two more years, per NPR.

Positions commonly voiced by Guterres, about 100 supportive states and humanitarian groups such as Stop Killer Robots.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • The talks ran Monday to Friday at a Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons meeting in Geneva and closed Saturday, Nov. 21, per CBC. Russia resisted binding commitments, per CBC. Reuters, as cited by Business Upturn, reported that Russia was not alone.
  • States agreed to 'elaborate' a possible accord on lethal autonomous weapons by 2028, per CBC. The convention has 128 parties, per Business Upturn.
  • Around 100 countries favored moving quickly toward binding rules, per Reuters as cited by Business Upturn.
  • UN disarmament chief Izumi Nakamitsu called the outcome a 'significant achievement' but acknowledged a 'glass half full or half empty' scenario, per CBC. NPR says the compromise leaves such weapons without binding rules.

Sourced from CBC News, NPR (via NPR Illinois) and Business Upturn, November 22, 2026.

3. Nvidia Posts Record Quarter Without Huang as Scandal and China Scrutiny Weigh

Strong results and higher guidance met a CEO vacancy, a board investigation and a House inquiry, and the stock kept falling.

GROWTH-FOCUSED
RISK-FOCUSED
GROWTH vs RISK
Growth-focused view
  • Revenue more than doubled to $115.8 billion and beat estimates. Fourth-quarter guidance of about $130 billion topped consensus.
  • Interim CEO Colette Kress says it is 'business as usual' and that customers and partners are behind the company.
  • Nvidia forecast 75% sales growth for fiscal 2028 and raised its buyback budget, per Bloomberg.
  • Nvidia assumes no China data-center revenue in its outlook, so guidance does not depend on the contested market. At about 15 times trailing earnings, the shares trade at their lowest multiple since at least 2014, per 24/7 Wall St.

Positions commonly voiced by Nvidia management and bullish analysts quoted by CNBC and Yahoo Finance.

Risk-focused view
  • The board cites an 'ongoing investigation,' and the chairman says it does not intend to reinstate Huang. The company has no permanent CEO.
  • House committee chairs want answers on a 'potential breach of our national security' tied to chip access and a suspected Chinese intelligence asset.
  • Nvidia's legal exposure over China access is a concern, and the company has warned chip buyers of legal risks, per CNBC.
  • Some customers lack investment-grade ratings and must line up their own demand for the capacity they buy, per Bloomberg. That adds risk to the AI spending cycle.

Positions commonly voiced by House committee chairs, governance-minded investors and cautious analysts.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Q3 revenue was $115.84 billion against $109.10 billion expected. Adjusted EPS was $2.68 against $2.48 expected, per CNBC. Fourth-quarter guidance was about $130 billion against a $125.1 billion consensus, per CNBC and LSEG.
  • CBS News reported profit of $65.3 billion, up 105% year over year. Interim CEO Colette Kress led her first earnings call after Huang resigned.
  • Huang will give a closed-door interview to three House committees on Dec. 1. General counsel Tim Teter follows on Dec. 2, per Euronews. Trump said 'Nobody's proven that she's a spy,' per The Washington Sun.
  • Nvidia closed at $125.53 Friday, 48% below its record, per 24/7 Wall St. It fell 23% for the week, its worst since October 2008, per Yahoo Finance. Chair Harvey Jones said Huang would not return, per CNBC.

Sourced from CNBC, CBS News, Euronews and Bloomberg, November 22, 2026.
