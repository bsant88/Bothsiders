1. AI Sell-Off Drags Nasdaq Below Its 2026 Starting Point

Stocks fell sharply Friday as Nvidia dropped on a reported SEC accounting probe, and investors are split on whether it is a reset or a turn.

BULL
BEAR
BULL vs BEAR
Bull view
  • Nvidia's latest quarterly report was another strong one. The sell-off is about sentiment and headlines, not demand.
  • An SEC inquiry is not a finding of wrongdoing, and many inquiries close without action.
  • Corrections after a big run-up can reset valuations. Defensive sectors such as healthcare rose, which suggests rotation more than panic.
  • Consumer data has held up. Gap's stock jumped 12% and its CEO called shoppers resilient but discerning.

Positions commonly voiced by dip-buying strategists and AI-sector optimists.

Bear view
  • Nvidia has lost about $2.7 trillion in value since its Oct. 6 record, with CEO turmoil and accounting questions on top.
  • Concerns over AI financing and profits are spreading beyond one stock. 58% of Nasdaq-100 members are in a bear market.
  • The Fed is expected to raise rates at its Dec. 8-9 meeting. Higher rates pressure richly valued tech stocks.
  • New U.S. curbs on remote chip access by Chinese firms add risk to chipmakers' sales.

Positions commonly voiced by valuation-focused analysts and rate-sensitive market skeptics.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • The Nasdaq closed down 5.28% Friday, erasing its 2026 gains. The S&P 500 fell 3.7% and the Dow fell 1.93% (CBC).
  • The VIX closed at its highest level since April 2025, according to Fox Business.
  • Reuters reported, citing two people briefed, that the SEC is probing Nvidia's accounting. The SEC declined to comment, and Reuters could not determine when the probe began or what it covers.
  • Nvidia fell about 13-14% Friday, according to Reuters and Yahoo Finance. AP reported that Fed minutes show nearly all officials expect a quarter-point hike in December.

2. Senate Rejects Iran War Powers Resolution 48-51

The first Senate vote on the war since the Nov. 3 midterms fell short, with three Republicans joining Democrats.

CONSERVATIVE
LIBERAL
BOTH SIDES
Conservative view
  • The War Powers Resolution's concurrent-resolution process is unconstitutional, a position administrations of both parties have taken.
  • A concurrent resolution does not go to the president and carries no force of law. Supporters of the vote are seen as seeking a political signal.
  • Directing a withdrawal in the middle of a conflict could weaken the U.S. position while the fighting remains unsettled.
  • Sen. Thom Tillis, who backed an earlier effort, voted no. He said the push partly reflects a failure to explain the threat from Iran.

Positions commonly voiced by Republican senators and administration allies.

Liberal view
  • Voters named the war and its toll on the economy among their top concerns in the midterms, and Congress should respond.
  • The war is unauthorized, and Congress has not voted to approve it.
  • The cost is rising. A Pentagon budget official put it at about $48.3 billion as of Nov. 13.
  • Sen. Tammy Duckworth said that nine months in, defunding the war is the only way to force an end. Reps. Ro Khanna and Yassamin Ansari said they would oppose funding that does not bar offensive operations.

Positions commonly voiced by Democratic lawmakers and war-powers advocates.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • The vote on H.Con.Res. 93 was 48-51. Republicans Collins, Murkowski and Paul voted yes. Democrat John Fetterman voted no, and Sen. Chris Murphy did not vote (CBS News, Roll Call).
  • The House passed the resolution in September by 220-204, according to Anadolu.
  • The Senate approved a similar resolution 50-48 on June 23 (Hans India/IANS, CBS News). Democrats have forced more than a dozen war powers votes since the war began in late February, per CBS News.
  • Pentagon deputy comptroller Arthur Miller told the Senate Armed Services Committee the war had cost about $48.3 billion as of Nov. 13 (Newsweek). Democrats are not set to control the Senate until January.

Sourced from CBS News, Roll Call, Newsweek and Middle East Monitor (Anadolu), November 21, 2026.

3. Trump Threatens 'Tremendous Tax' on Overseas AI Data Centers

The president gave no details, and the threat lands amid public skepticism of data centers and a new Senate bill on their power costs.

INNOVATION
OVERSIGHT
INNOVATION vs OVERSIGHT
Innovation view
  • Trump says keeping data centers and their jobs in the U.S. is needed to stay ahead of China in AI.
  • Communities can negotiate tax breaks, lower utility bills and direct payments, so the buildout can bring local gains.
  • Faster permitting would help the U.S. build the power and grid capacity that AI needs. Trump praised the Senate's permitting bill.
  • Industry voices argue a broad levy could raise costs for firms serving customers abroad. Google's planned $15 billion Finland investment is one example Trump has criticized.

Positions commonly voiced by the Trump administration, AI-industry groups and permitting-reform backers.

Oversight view
  • A majority of Americans oppose large data centers, and AP linked voter concerns about them to the Republican midterm losses.
  • A tax announced without a rate, scope or notice to officials shows why clear, written rules are needed.
  • The Senate bill would make data centers pay a larger share of grid and infrastructure costs, so ratepayers do not carry them.
  • Democrats such as Sen. Mark Warner are pushing AI guardrails, including a bill requiring disclosure of AI-related financing by public companies.

Positions commonly voiced by Democratic lawmakers, consumer advocates and data center critics.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Trump said Friday that companies building AI data centers abroad would face a 'tremendous tax.' He gave no rate, scope or timeline, and said he had not told his own officials (AP, ABC News, IBTimes).
  • A Commerce Department rule that took effect Thursday sets licensing requirements for some advanced chip exports tied to overseas data centers serving China-linked firms (IBTimes).
  • The Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act 62-33 on Nov. 19. It speeds energy permitting and makes data centers pay more of infrastructure costs, and it still needs House passage (CNBC, HuffPost via Yahoo).
  • Warner's bill on AI financing disclosures was reported by CNBC alone and was not independently confirmed here.

Sourced from AP via Yahoo News, ABC News, IBTimes and CNBC, November 21, 2026.
