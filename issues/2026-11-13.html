1. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang Resigns Amid Probe Over Relationship With Alleged Chinese Spy

Nvidia's board named CFO Colette Kress interim CEO as the stock fell and markets weighed what the exit means for the AI trade.

GROWTH-FOCUSED
RISK-FOCUSED
GROWTH vs RISK
Growth-focused view
  • Nvidia is still selling AI chips at a fast pace whoever is on stage, and analysts say it remains the industry leader.
  • The stock recovered about two-thirds of its early drop, which suggests investors see the business as intact.
  • The board says operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap will continue without interruption.
  • A search covering internal and external candidates could bring in new leadership for the next phase of AI demand.

Positions commonly voiced by Nvidia's board, chip-sector analysts and AI-boom investors.

Risk-focused view
  • Losing the co-founder and public face of the company leaves a leadership gap at one of the market's largest weights.
  • The FBI and the board's outside counsel are examining the relationship, and lawmakers from both parties want more answers.
  • Analysts told CNBC the exit could trigger a 20% correction in the stock.
  • AI-linked names such as CoreWeave fell, and some analysts warn the exit adds to pressure on AI valuations.

Positions commonly voiced by national-security-minded lawmakers and analysts who warn of an AI-stock bubble.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Nvidia's Nov. 12 filing says Huang resigned as president, CEO and director effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO and Harvey C. Jones is the first board chair.
  • CNBC reported, citing an unnamed person familiar, that the board decided the night before to oust Huang. Nvidia's filing describes a resignation accepted Nov. 11.
  • Nvidia fell nearly 14% at the open on Nov. 12 and closed down 4.3%. The S&P 500 closed down 0.4% at 7,592 (Kiplinger; AP via BNN Bloomberg).
  • The board hired Wachtell Lipton to investigate (CNBC). Variety reports Trump confirmed the FBI is also investigating the relationship.

Sourced from NVIDIA Form 8-K / press release (SEC), CNBC, Kiplinger and AP via BNN Bloomberg, November 13, 2026.

2. Hot October CPI Keeps a December Fed Rate Hike on the Table

Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above forecasts, and a Fed governor has floated a half-point move.

BULL
BEAR
BULL vs BEAR
Bull view
  • Monthly CPI slowed to 0.4% from September's 0.6%, so the peak pace of price gains may have passed.
  • A strong October jobs report shows the economy can absorb higher rates.
  • Several economists expect only a quarter-point hike in December, not a half-point.
  • Stocks were on track for their best week since early August despite the Nvidia shock.

Positions commonly voiced by economists who expect gradual Fed tightening and by market optimists.

Bear view
  • Core CPI rose 0.3% for a third straight month, above the 0.2% consensus, so inflation looks persistent.
  • Bank of America says the Fed looks behind the curve and calls the October hold a policy mistake.
  • BofA says prices excluding rent accelerated to 5.5% in October from 1% in July.
  • A larger hike would raise borrowing costs for households, and AI-linked stock volatility adds to the risk.

Positions commonly voiced by Bank of America strategists and inflation hawks.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • October CPI rose 0.4% against a 0.3% forecast. Core CPI rose 0.3% against a 0.2% consensus. September was 0.6% (Benzinga, citing BLS).
  • BLS published no annual rate because October 2025 prices were not collected during last year's government shutdown (Benzinga).
  • Fed Governor Christopher Waller said a 50-basis-point hike should be considered in December if core inflation came in near 0.3% or higher. The policy rate is 3.75%-4.00% and the next decision is Dec. 9 (Benzinga).
  • Market odds differ by source. Benzinga reports a 99% chance of a December hike. InvestmentNews cites CME FedWatch at 84.7% after the release, down from 98.6% earlier that day, for a move to 4%-4.25%.

Sourced from Benzinga, InvestmentNews, TheStreet and CNBC, November 13, 2026.

3. Nonprofits Sue Five AI Developers, Seeking Court Freeze on Frontier Model Training

A San Francisco lawsuit asks a judge to halt next-generation models at Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceX's AI unit.

INNOVATION
OVERSIGHT
INNOVATION vs OVERSIGHT
Innovation view
  • A court-ordered pause is an extraordinary remedy, and an unfair-competition statute is not a tool for setting AI policy.
  • The U.S. is racing to stay ahead of China, and a unilateral halt could cede the lead to Beijing.
  • Safety work happens inside the labs, and a freeze would also stop progress on safety tools and benefits.
  • Rules should come from Congress and expert agencies, not injunctions from a single state court.

Positions commonly voiced by AI developers, administration officials and industry groups that stress competition with China.

Oversight view
  • The companies' own leaders have warned that advanced AI could threaten humanity, and the suit uses those statements.
  • The complaint cites incidents of AI-driven cyberattacks and models acting outside controlled environments.
  • Voluntary restraint has not produced binding safeguards, and no comprehensive federal AI law exists.
  • Plaintiffs argue a pause need not hand China the lead if Washington sets global rules and opens talks with Beijing.

Positions voiced by the plaintiff groups and AI-safety advocates.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Four nonprofits (Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative, Alliance for Secure AI) sued in San Francisco Superior Court under California's Unfair Competition Law (AI Weekly). Outlets give the filing date as Nov. 11 (AI Weekly) or Nov. 12 (Crypto Briefing).
  • Meta declined to comment, per reports. The other defendants had not responded to requests for comment at the time of the reports.
  • The suit seeks an emergency injunction on next-generation models until the firms prove human control (AI Weekly, Al Jazeera). It also calls for federal independent evaluators, strict liability and a new AI regulator (AI Weekly).
  • Al Jazeera notes that AI firms and President Trump have repeatedly signaled the U.S. is racing to beat China. A policy tracker says no comprehensive federal AI bill has passed either chamber.

Sourced from Al Jazeera, AI Weekly and Crypto Briefing, November 13, 2026.

4. White House Counsel Warns House Democrats That Aggressive Investigations Could Be Criminal

A letter to incoming Oversight Chairman Robert Garcia previews clashes once Democrats take control of Congress in January.

CONSERVATIVE
LIBERAL
BOTH SIDES
Conservative view
  • Congressional subpoenas need a legitimate legislative purpose, and the White House says some planned ones would be unlawful.
  • Oversight can be used for partisan harassment, and the administration is entitled to defend its executive-branch confidentiality.
  • Democrats complained of partisan probes when Republicans ran Congress, so the standard should apply to both parties.
  • Preparing a legal defense now, including adding staff in the counsel's office, is ordinary prudence.

Positions commonly voiced by White House officials and Republican commentators.

Liberal view
  • Congress's investigative authority comes from Article I of the Constitution, not from White House permission.
  • Threatening to prosecute lawmakers over subpoenas is intimidation meant to chill legitimate oversight.
  • Critics point to the firing of an attorney general and prosecutors, and to resignations at the Justice Department, as reasons oversight is needed.
  • Democrats will control the power of the purse and can use it as leverage in disputes with the White House.

Positions commonly voiced by House Democrats and progressive commentators such as The New Republic.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • White House counsel Will Scharf sent the letter to Rep. Robert Garcia on Wednesday, Nov. 11. PBS reports it warned that aggressive oversight 'may cross the line into blatant criminality.'
  • NBC News reports the White House began building a legal defense before Election Day, with hires in the counsel's office. Aides have also hired personal lawyers.
  • Garcia responded that the Constitution gives Congress wide latitude to conduct investigations (NBC News).
  • Democrats take control of Congress in January, per Reuters. PBS notes the warning came amid a wave of resignations within the Justice Department.

Sourced from NBC News, PBS NewsHour, Reuters via Investing.com and The New Republic, November 13, 2026.

5. Trump Pulls Vance From Iran Talks, Says 'No Deal Needed'

The president reassigned the vice president to a fraud task force a day after reports Vance sought direct talks with a senior Iranian official.

CONSERVATIVE
LIBERAL
BOTH SIDES
Conservative view
  • The U.S. holds leverage and does not need to chase an agreement, as Trump argues.
  • Diplomacy has been tried: April talks in Pakistan fizzled and a June tentative deal collapsed when Iran failed to meet commitments.
  • Pressing on negotiations rewards an adversary that has not honored earlier terms.
  • Vance can be more useful leading the anti-fraud task force that Trump says could help balance the budget.

Positions commonly voiced by Trump and allied conservative outlets.

Liberal view
  • Ending the diplomatic track removes the main off-ramp from a war that has raised gas prices.
  • Vance himself blamed the war for the GOP's midterm losses, and critics see the reassignment as punishment for saying so.
  • Reassigning the vice president to fraud in Democratic-run cities looks politically motivated rather than a policy rationale.
  • With Trump also saying he may use force 'right now' if allies or ships are threatened, escalation risk is higher without talks.

Positions commonly voiced by Democrats and critics of the Iran war.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Trump announced Nov. 12 on Truth Social that he was pulling Vance from Iran negotiations and asked him to focus on alleged crime and corruption in Democrat-run cities and states (Daily Caller, Yahoo News).
  • The move followed a Wall Street Journal report that Vance was considering meeting a top Iranian security official. The Journal report was not seen directly and is cited by Yahoo News and the Washington Times.
  • Vance blamed the Iran war above all for Republican midterm losses in a post-election post on X. Yahoo News reports gas prices spiked during the conflict.
  • Anadolu's morning briefing reports Trump said he may use military force against Iran 'right now' if U.S. allies and ships are threatened.

Sourced from Yahoo News, The Washington Times, Daily Caller and Anadolu Agency, November 13, 2026.

6. Raiders Trade Kirk Cousins to Vikings, Hand Starting Job to Rookie Fernando Mendoza

The deadline-day deal sends the veteran back to Minnesota for a 2027 third-round pick and starts the Mendoza era in Las Vegas.

TAKE
COUNTER-TAKE
TAKE vs COUNTER-TAKE
Take
  • It is a win-win: CBS Sports calls everyone involved a winner.
  • Vegas turned a placeholder QB into a top-100 pick and gets to play the No. 1 overall pick now, as CBS Sports argues.
  • The Vikings had to act after a two-game skid, and Cousins knows the offense and has Justin Jefferson.
  • The Sporting News gave Minnesota an A+ and the Raiders an A-, calling it a rare deal that helps both teams.

Positions voiced by CBS Sports and Sporting News trade-grade writers.

Counter-take
  • The Raiders may have undersold: The Sporting News says they might have extracted more from a desperate Vikings team, and one outlet argued for a second-rounder.
  • Cousins had the Raiders at 4-5 and on the playoff bubble, so moving him gives up wins now.
  • A rookie starter adds volatility, ESPN notes, and Mendoza debuts against Seattle's stingy defense.
  • CBS Sports gave the Vikings only a B-, saying Cousins cannot fix everything.

Positions voiced by trade critics, including ESPN and uSports commentators.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Minnesota acquired Cousins from Las Vegas on Nov. 10 for a 2027 third-round pick (NFL.com). The Vikings were 6-2 and on a two-game losing streak (Sporting News via Yahoo).
  • With the Raiders, Cousins threw for 2,340 yards, 20 touchdowns and 8 interceptions in a 4-5 start (NFL.com).
  • Raiders GM John Spytek said he did not shop Cousins and that Minnesota reached out Tuesday morning (NFL.com).
  • Mendoza was named the starter and makes his first start Sunday at home against the Seattle Seahawks (ESPN).

Sourced from NFL.com, CBS Sports, Sporting News via Yahoo Sports and ESPN, November 13, 2026.
