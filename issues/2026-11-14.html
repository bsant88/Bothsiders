CHIPS & CHINA

1. Nvidia CEO's exit puts chip export controls under scrutiny

Jensen Huang resigned after reports of ties to an alleged Chinese spy. Congress now wants to know why Commerce did not blacklist Megaspeed, an Nvidia partner.

INNOVATION
OVERSIGHT
INNOVATION vs OVERSIGHT
Innovation view
  • One executive's scandal is not evidence that the whole chip-export policy failed.
  • Selling lower-grade chips abroad keeps rivals such as Huawei from gaining market share.
  • Fast, flexible licensing keeps U.S. AI leadership and revenue at home.
  • Hearings should look at specific diversion by shell companies, not penalize the industry broadly.

Positions commonly voiced by chip-industry executives and administration AI advisers who favor broad chip sales abroad.

Oversight view
  • Megaspeed was suspected of diverting advanced chips to China, yet it stayed off the Entity List for months.
  • Treasury, State, War and USTR favored listing it, and Commerce held the pen. Republican and Democratic senators want answers.
  • Policy set by a close Nvidia-Commerce relationship risks putting company interests ahead of national security.
  • Export rules need independent enforcement, not loopholes or discretion tied to trade talks with Beijing.

Positions voiced by Treasury Secretary Bessent, Sens. Banks and Warren, and other lawmakers in both parties.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Nvidia said Nov. 12 that Huang resigned as CEO effective immediately. The Washington Post reported CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO and that Nvidia stock fell 4% that day.
  • The FBI, Congress and Nvidia's board each opened investigations. China denies the spy allegations. Attorney General Blanche said the Justice Department has not determined whether Alice Huang was a spy (CNN).
  • Commerce Secretary Lutnick agreed to testify to the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 10. Chair Tim Scott also asked Huang to appear Dec. 3 (CNBC, CBS).
  • Megaspeed bought more than $4 billion of Nvidia chips, per AP. It said in December there was 'no evidence' of illicit exports. The Times report that Bessent confronted Lutnick in the Situation Room is unconfirmed; Bessent declined to comment.

Sourced from CBS News, CNBC, The Washington Post and AP via The Hill, November 14, 2026.

INFLATION & THE FED

2. Wholesale inflation hits 6.5% as Fed hike odds firm up

October producer prices rose faster than any year since 2022, but came in slightly below forecasts. A December rate hike is widely expected.

BULL
BEAR
BULL vs BEAR
Bull view
  • Monthly producer prices rose only 0.3%, below the 0.4% economists expected and far below September's 1.4%.
  • The annual reading of 6.5% was under the 6.6% forecast, and wholesale diesel fell 7.9% from September.
  • Strong hiring and low jobless claims give the economy room to absorb a hike.
  • A December quarter-point move is already priced in. All three major indexes posted weekly gains.

Positions commonly voiced by market strategists who read the data as cooling at the margin.

Bear view
  • The 6.5% annual rate is the highest since November 2022, and October consumer prices came in hotter than expected.
  • Price pressure is broadening across everyday goods and services, not just energy.
  • Fed officials are sounding more aggressive, with talk of back-to-back hikes and a possible half-point move.
  • Higher rates hit loans and credit lines, and AI-heavy stocks are already swinging sharply.

Positions commonly voiced by Fed hawks and economists who see inflation as persistent.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported producer prices up 6.5% in the 12 months to October, versus 6.3% in September. The monthly gain was 0.3% after an upwardly revised 1.4% in September (The Standard, Investing.com).
  • The U.S. added 165,000 jobs in October and unemployment fell to 4.1% (American Banker). On Nov. 13 the S&P 500 closed down 0.4% at 7,561.67 (AFP).
  • New York Fed's Williams said inflation is 'far too high.' Governor Waller said a 50-basis-point hike may be needed (Yahoo Finance).
  • The Fed held its rate at 3.75%-4.00% in October. Its next decision is Dec. 9, and markets are split between a 25 and a 50 basis-point hike (The Standard, StockTitan).

Sourced from The Standard (HK), Investing.com, Yahoo Finance and AFP via France 24, November 14, 2026.

OVERSIGHT FIGHT

3. White House warns Democrats their planned probes could be criminal

Counsel Will Scharf's letter to Rep. Robert Garcia previews a clash over congressional oversight when Democrats take the House in January.

CONSERVATIVE
LIBERAL
BOTH SIDES
Conservative view
  • Congressional investigations need a legitimate legislative purpose and cannot take over prosecutorial powers held by the executive and the courts.
  • Garcia's promise to go after 'the crooks, the con men, and the Trump crime family' and his 'lawyer up' message show intent to harass rather than legislate.
  • Threats meant to deter people from public service, or subpoenas meant to instill fear, can cross into criminality.
  • The administration has a duty to defend its officials, and the Justice Department says it will represent them before Congress.

Positions voiced by White House counsel Will Scharf, Attorney General Todd Blanche and President Trump.

Liberal view
  • Article I gives Congress wide latitude to investigate, and it needs no permission from the White House.
  • A warning that oversight could be criminal is an attempt to chill a coequal branch. Garcia said the president 'is clearly hiding something.'
  • It is unclear how the Democratic statements cited could violate federal criminal law, and some legal scholars say the letter could help Democrats show intent to obstruct.
  • Examining how Trump and his family have grown wealthier since he returned to office is classic oversight.

Positions voiced by Rep. Robert Garcia, incoming Oversight chair, and House Democrats.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • The letter is dated Monday, Nov. 9. It says Democratic probes 'may cross the line into blatant criminality' and rests on two Supreme Court decisions (AP, Sullivan & Cromwell).
  • Trump called the planned probes 'extortion' on Nov. 7 and ordered the Justice Department to look into it. Blanche said threats could cross a line into criminal conduct (Sullivan & Cromwell, AP).
  • Garcia said the committee had not formally received the letter as of Wednesday. Republican chair James Comer said he had not read it and called the panel 'very independent' (ABC, AP via MPR).
  • Politico, as cited by Inquisitr, reported the Justice Department normally handles congressional oversight, making a White House counsel letter unusual. Time News reported Blanche said Thursday the department has no investigations into lawmakers' words; that claim comes from one outlet.

Sourced from AP via PBS NewsHour, ABC News, Sullivan & Cromwell and AP via MPR News, November 14, 2026.
