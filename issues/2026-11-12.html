OVERSIGHT FIGHT

1. White House warns House Democrats their planned probes could be criminal

A letter from White House counsel Will Scharf to incoming Oversight chair Robert Garcia sets up an early clash over congressional investigative power.

CONSERVATIVE
LIBERAL
BOTH SIDES
Conservative view
  • Congress may investigate only to serve a legitimate legislative purpose, and subpoenas used to harass officials fall outside that power.
  • Garcia's 'lawyer up' message after the election reads as a pledge to target officials rather than to write laws.
  • Officials have the right to push back early on probes they see as designed to deter people from public service.
  • Republicans have long invoked the legislative-purpose limit, so the White House is applying a familiar standard.

Positions commonly voiced by White House officials, Trump allies and congressional Republicans.

Liberal view
  • Article I gives Congress broad, inherent investigative authority that needs no permission from the White House.
  • Voters handed Democrats the House in the midterms, and Garcia says that is a mandate for accountability.
  • Threatening criminal inquiries against lawmakers is itself intimidation meant to chill legitimate oversight.
  • Critics note the administration and its allies have made similar threats against Democrats, and that obstructing Congress is a crime.

Positions commonly voiced by Rep. Robert Garcia, House Democrats and critics of the administration.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • The letter was first reported by Punchbowl News. It says Democratic probes 'may cross the line into blatant criminality' and that Congress may investigate only for a legislative purpose.
  • Outlets differ on timing. AP-based reports and Punchbowl say the letter was dated or sent Monday. PBS News and CNN describe it as sent Wednesday (Nov. 11).
  • Garcia is in line to chair the House Oversight Committee when Democrats take control in January. The Los Angeles Times reported his office had not received the letter as of Wednesday morning.
  • Garcia responded that Congress's authority comes from Article I, not from White House permission. He also said the letter suggests Trump has something to hide.

Sourced from PBS News, Punchbowl News, NY1 (AP) and CNN, November 12, 2026.

IRAN WAR

2. Trump says US has won Iran war but wants 'total victory' as Hormuz attacks continue

At Arlington on Veterans Day, Trump declared victory while saying the conflict is not over, and Iran's Guards reported more tanker incidents.

CONSERVATIVE
LIBERAL
BOTH SIDES
Conservative view
  • Iran has been waging war on America since 1979, and Trump is the first president to confront it directly.
  • Settling for a partial win would leave the threat in place, so pressing for total victory is the right standard.
  • Open-ended economic and naval pressure, including the blockade, keeps Iran's leverage shrinking without new large-scale strikes.
  • Threats against senior US officials show the regime remains hostile and why resolve matters.

Positions commonly voiced by Trump, administration officials and congressional Republicans.

Liberal view
  • Declaring victory while tankers still burn in Hormuz shows the stated end state has not been reached.
  • Oil prices and war costs are hitting households, and analysts tie the Republican midterm losses partly to them.
  • Saying the war is 'effectively over' while seeking allied help on the strait shows no clear diplomatic exit.
  • Congress should demand a defined mission, cost accounting and a negotiated settlement rather than open-ended brinkmanship.

Positions commonly voiced by Democratic lawmakers, foreign-policy critics and commentators skeptical of the war's costs.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • On Nov. 11 at Arlington National Cemetery, Trump said the US 'won' the war and wants total victory rather than a 93% victory, per CBS News and ABC News.
  • On Monday, Trump said the war was 'militarily, effectively over' and the US was 'keeping it the status quo', as reported by Newsweek.
  • CBS News reported that Iran's Revolutionary Guard said Thursday two tankers carrying over four million barrels caught fire in an 'accident' on an unauthorized Hormuz route. This is Iran's own claim.
  • Iran's parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf again urged Washington to accept Iran's seven demands for reopening Hormuz and resuming talks, per Seoul Economic Daily.

Sourced from CBS News, ABC News, Seoul Economic Daily and Newsweek, November 12, 2026.

INFLATION & RATES

3. Hot October CPI puts a December Fed rate hike in focus

Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above forecasts, while stocks fell Tuesday and rebounded Wednesday.

BULL
BEAR
BULL vs BEAR
Bull view
  • Headline inflation cooled to 0.4% in October from 0.6% in September, and energy contributed less to the rise.
  • A December hike is already priced in, so the data adds little new risk for markets.
  • Stocks recovered quickly: the S&P 500 rose 1.3% on Wednesday and sits within 2.6% of its record.
  • Strong AI-linked earnings, such as CoreWeave's results, show the corporate growth story is intact.

Positions commonly voiced by equity strategists and AI-trade optimists.

Bear view
  • October was the third straight month of outsized price increases, and core inflation at 0.3% was slightly above forecasts.
  • Chicago Fed's Austan Goolsbee called the report borderline disturbing, and said tariff effects have not faded and services inflation stays high.
  • Fed Governor Christopher Waller said a half-point hike should be on the table if core CPI runs around 0.3%.
  • Higher rates plus a Nvidia-led tech sell-off leave equity valuations exposed.

Positions commonly voiced by Fed officials worried about persistence, and by rate-hike and valuation skeptics.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • CBS News and CNBC report October CPI rose 0.4% month over month, above forecasts. The CNBC preview had put the consensus at 0.3% headline and 0.2% core. Core rose 0.3%.
  • CBS News says September's headline monthly gain was 0.6%. Next to September, October energy costs added less to inflation.
  • CNBC reported the S&P 500 fell 0.38% to 7,520.93 on Tuesday after the data. On Wednesday it rose 1.3% to 7,619.38, per AP via BNN Bloomberg.
  • Benzinga cited CME FedWatch pricing a Fed hike after the December meeting at 100% likelihood, with 86.2% odds for a 25-basis-point move.

Sourced from CBS News, CNBC and BNN Bloomberg (AP), November 12, 2026.

NVIDIA LEADERSHIP

4. Nvidia board reportedly preparing to oust CEO Jensen Huang

WSJ reports the board is weighing Huang's removal after two alleged affairs, one with a suspected Chinese spy. Nvidia has not confirmed it.

GROWTH-FOCUSED
RISK-FOCUSED
GROWTH vs RISK
Growth-focused view
  • Nvidia's franchise rests on AI demand, not on one executive, and a decisive board response can protect long-term value.
  • Investors who see the sell-off as overdone argue fundamentals, such as AI infrastructure spending, remain strong.
  • Chip stocks rallied Wednesday on CoreWeave's results, showing the AI trade can absorb shocks.
  • A thorough independent probe and orderly transition would reduce uncertainty over time.

Positions commonly voiced by AI-sector bulls and growth investors.

Risk-focused view
  • Huang has no named successor, so an abrupt removal creates execution risk at a roughly $4 trillion company.
  • The probe covers possible leaks of trade secrets and national-security exposure, which could invite regulatory and export-control consequences.
  • Chinese AI firms are reportedly rushing to secure overseas Nvidia-powered capacity in case Washington restricts remote chip use.
  • Analysts say damage to a founder-led brand is hard to reverse, and Nvidia's stock has already lost about $600 billion in value this week.

Positions commonly voiced by governance-minded analysts and national-security-focused investors.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • WSJ reported Wednesday, citing people familiar, that the board is preparing to oust Huang. The board could meet as soon as Wednesday. Reuters relayed the report and said Nvidia and Kimberly Powell did not immediately respond. The ouster rests on WSJ sourcing and was not independently confirmed.
  • The NYT reported a second relationship, with VP of healthcare Kimberly Powell, who reports to Huang. Huang earlier admitted the relationship with Alice Huang, whom US intelligence has said helped facilitate chip shipments to Chinese firms, per Reuters.
  • Nvidia's board launched an investigation of whether Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. Yahoo Finance describes it as a Wachtell Lipton-led probe.
  • Premarket drops are reported differently: Newsquawk says -5.5%, Yahoo Finance says -7.5%. Reuters said Nvidia lost about $600 billion in value this week. Trump called Huang 'an honorable guy'.

Sourced from Reuters via U.S. News, Reuters via Yahoo Finance, CNBC and Newsquawk, November 12, 2026.

AI RULES

5. New York opens AI safety registration as Democrats plan federal AI bills

State enforcement of the RAISE Act and a Democratic agenda for January mark a shift from a Congress that mostly promoted AI development.

INNOVATION
OVERSIGHT
INNOVATION vs OVERSIGHT
Innovation view
  • Mandatory testing, liability exposure and data-center limits can raise costs and delay product launches.
  • A state-by-state rulebook creates a patchwork that is hard for developers to navigate.
  • Some Wall Street brokerages say the risk of extreme data-center regulation under Democrats is overstated and the AI sell-off was overdone.
  • Slowing data-center build-out risks ceding ground to rivals; Trump has argued China will take any data center the US does not want.

Positions commonly voiced by AI developers, industry analysts and pro-growth investors.

Oversight view
  • A Reuters poll in October found most US voters think the administration and Congress are not taking AI risks seriously enough.
  • Voters in the 2026 elections backed slowing data-center construction over electricity-cost concerns, according to Reuters.
  • Independent testing, incident reporting and clear liability rules are basic accountability for the most powerful models.
  • Gov. Kathy Hochul says New York is building a model for sensible AI regulation that puts people above powerful technology.

Positions commonly voiced by Democratic lawmakers, state officials and AI-safety advocates.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • TechCrunch reports New York opened a registration portal Tuesday. Large frontier developers such as OpenAI and Anthropic must register by Jan. 1, 2027.
  • Penalties under the RAISE Act can reach $1 million for a first violation and $3 million for subsequent ones, per TechCrunch.
  • Reuters reported Nov. 6 that Democrats plan bills on data-center electricity costs, independent testing of advanced AI, and AI-company liability once they take Congress in January.
  • Reuters via Yahoo reported TD Cowen cut its odds for passage of a bipartisan permitting bill that would make large data centers pay more for grid upgrades to 25% from 40% before the election.

Sourced from TechCrunch, Reuters via U.S. News, The Globe and Mail (Reuters) and Reuters via Yahoo, November 12, 2026.

COACHING MOVE

6. Manchester United close in on USMNT coach Pochettino

ESPN reports United have contacted U.S. Soccer about the $10 million release clause as they prepare to replace Michael Carrick.

TAKE
COUNTER-TAKE
TAKE vs COUNTER-TAKE
Take
  • Pochettino is a proven Premier League manager with Tottenham and Chelsea on his record, and United need a reset after their worst start in decades.
  • The roughly $10 million clause is modest for a club of United's size, even with about $2 million more for his staff.
  • He has said he wants a return to the Premier League, so the USMNT is not holding back a reluctant coach.
  • The US has plenty of time before the 2030 World Cup to find a successor.

Positions commonly voiced by United-leaning pundits and reporters tracking the deal.

Counter-take
  • Leaving only months after signing an extension through 2030 undercuts the continuity that deal was meant to give the USMNT.
  • His USMNT record is modest: 21 wins in 45 games, nearly all against lower-ranked teams, per CBS Sports.
  • United previously looked at Pochettino but went with Erik ten Hag, so the club's coaching decisions have been inconsistent.
  • Firing Carrick this soon and paying a buyout repeats a cycle of instability, and some United fans have turned on the owners.

Positions commonly voiced by USMNT-focused commentators and critics of United's ownership.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • ESPN, citing sources, reports United contacted U.S. Soccer ahead of triggering the $10 million clause. Executives are set to meet Carrick Thursday, with the expectation he will be relieved of his duties.
  • Pochettino signed an extension in early August keeping him through the 2030 World Cup, per Yahoo Sports. The deal reportedly includes the release clause.
  • ESPN says U.S. Soccer expects Pochettino to oversee two Concacaf Nations League quarterfinal games against Haiti, on Nov. 14 and Nov. 17.
  • His record is reported inconsistently: CBS Sports says 21 wins in 45 games, while Yahoo Sports lists 21 wins, 1 draw and 13 losses in 35 games. CBS also notes The Athletic reported United had not yet contacted U.S. Soccer.

Sourced from ESPN, CBS Sports and Yahoo Sports, November 12, 2026.
