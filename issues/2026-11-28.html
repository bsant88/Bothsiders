1. Trump order routes contractor replies to Congress through the White House

Executive Order 14438 arrives weeks before Democrats take over Congress and plan investigations.

CONSERVATIVE
LIBERAL
BOTH SIDES
Conservative view
  • Executive privilege is a constitutional protection, and contractors often hold confidential executive-branch material that the president must be able to review first.
  • Congress needs a valid legislative purpose, and the order says requests that intrude on core executive functions fall outside that.
  • Presidents of both parties have invoked privilege to resist congressional inquiries, so the order extends an old practice to a new setting.
  • Incoming Democratic investigators have signaled probes of contracts, so a coordinated process guards against what the White House calls harassment of private companies.

Positions commonly voiced by the White House, administration allies and defenders of executive privilege.

Liberal view
  • The order lets the president screen what Congress learns about his own administration's contracts, which critics say defeats the purpose of oversight.
  • Companies are caught between Congress and the executive, and the White House warns of contract termination if they do not comply.
  • Only the executive branch can invoke privilege. Extending it to contractors' communications is an untested legal theory.
  • The timing, weeks before Democrats take the majority, suggests the aim is to shield the administration from scrutiny, not to protect confidentiality.

Positions commonly voiced by congressional Democrats such as Rep. Jamie Raskin and Rep. Robert Garcia, and by civil liberties and oversight advocates.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Trump signed EO 14438 on Nov. 20, 2026. It requires federal contractors and subcontractors to notify the White House and contracting agencies right away of congressional requests (ABC News, Wiley).
  • Contractors must follow White House or agency instructions to withhold communications deemed privileged. The White House warned that noncompliance could lead to contract termination (ABC News, AP via NPR).
  • A White House fact sheet calls the order protection of executive privileges from congressional overreach (CBS News, Government Executive).
  • Garcia called it an attack on oversight, and Raskin called it unprecedented and unlawful (AP via NPR, HuffPost). Wiley notes only the executive branch can invoke privilege, and the order's reach remains open to legal dispute.

Sourced from ABC News, NPR (AP), Wiley and Government Executive, November 28, 2026.

2. Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang after Trump presses board; Congress demands answers

The chip giant's CEO returned two weeks after resigning over a scandal with national security overtones.

INNOVATION
OVERSIGHT
INNOVATION vs OVERSIGHT
Innovation view
  • Huang is central to Nvidia's AI chip leadership, and his return steadies a company whose stock fell sharply after he left.
  • Nvidia's value carries wide weight in the U.S. economy and AI race, so a prolonged leadership vacuum is costly.
  • The administration says its review found no national security risk, and Trump has called Huang exonerated.
  • Personal conduct and national security export questions can be handled by continuing probes without sidelining the executive who built the company.

Positions commonly voiced by Trump, administration officials and AI-industry and market-stability advocates.

Oversight view
  • A president personally lobbying a company board to reinstate a CEO under scrutiny is highly unusual and invites questions about motives.
  • Huang's relationship with a woman U.S. intelligence believes is a Chinese spy raises unresolved questions about export compliance.
  • Lawmakers in both parties say reinstatement does not answer the questions, and they want hearings and stronger chip export controls.
  • Critics want to know what legal assurances the administration offered the board and whether anyone traded stock before the announcement.

Positions commonly voiced by Democratic lawmakers such as Sens. Warren, Wyden and Schumer, and by some Republicans such as Sen. Kevin Cramer and Rep. Don Bacon.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Nvidia's board announced Nov. 23, 2026 that Huang was reinstated as president and CEO, effective immediately, about two weeks after he resigned (NBC News).
  • Trump told CNBC "I convinced them." CBS News reported Trump was privately concerned about the U.S. economy if Nvidia's value collapsed. CBC reported tech stocks surged Monday after the announcement.
  • Huang resigned after revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman U.S. intelligence believes is a Chinese spy (NBC News). Nvidia said the board acted after input from senior levels of the U.S. government.
  • AP reported bipartisan anger in Congress, with senior Republicans mostly quiet. CBS reported Nvidia's board chair had told investors a week earlier that Huang would not return. CBC quoted a tech adviser saying reinstatement is not exoneration.

Sourced from NBC News, CBS News, PBS NewsHour (AP) and CNBC, November 28, 2026.

3. Bills beat Chiefs 13-10 on Thanksgiving amid replay and clock disputes

An overturned catch and extra seconds on the final drive have fans and analysts arguing over what decided the game.

TAKE
COUNTER-TAKE
TAKE vs COUNTER-TAKE
Take
  • The league office overturned Rashee Rice's catch without clear and obvious evidence, as Andy Reid argued, and that call cost Kansas City a drive in a tied game.
  • The on-field crew ruled it a catch, so the New York overrule removed a decision the officials on the field had made.
  • Analysts say the clock was reset after multiple plays in the final 26 seconds, which gave Buffalo enough time for its 57-yard field goal.
  • Together the calls swung a game that decided the AFC's top seed, so the league owes a public explanation.

Positions commonly voiced by Chiefs coach Andy Reid, Brittany Mahomes, ESPN's Mina Kimes, former players and Chiefs fans.

Counter-take
  • Replay reviewed whether Rice kept control through the ground, and the league office ruled he lost it, so the ruling was within the rules.
  • Counts of added clock time vary, and CBS Sports said Tyler Bass would still have had a shot with one second left.
  • CBS also said it cannot be known whether the extra time cost the Chiefs the win.
  • Kansas City's own choices mattered, including the fumbled snap and a conservative approach on its last two possessions, which fans and commentators criticized.

Positions commonly voiced by neutral analysts, Bills fans and critics of Kansas City's late-game management.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Buffalo won 13-10 on Nov. 26, 2026 when Tyler Bass kicked a 57-yard field goal on the final play (CBS Sports).
  • On third-and-10, the officials ruled an 11-yard Rice catch, and the league's New York office overturned it, forcing a punt (NBC Sports). NBC timed it at 1:51 left and The Spun at about 1:47. Reid said it was not clear and obvious.
  • Chiefs had the ball at their own 11 with 26 seconds left and Buffalo held all three timeouts (CBS Sports). Mahomes fell on a dropped snap and Buffalo took a timeout.
  • Estimates of extra clock time differ. ProFootball Talk counted four seconds and CBS Sports counted three (via Yahoo Sports). Analyst Warren Sharp alleged seven seconds across two plays (Heavy).

Sourced from CBS Sports, NBC Sports, Yahoo Sports (The Spun) and Heavy, November 28, 2026.
