NVIDIA

1. Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang after Trump call to board; Congress presses ahead with hearings

The board reversed course about two weeks after his resignation, and lawmakers in both parties say questions about China-bound chips remain.

INNOVATION
OVERSIGHT
INNOVATION vs OVERSIGHT
Innovation view
  • Huang leads the company at the center of U.S. AI chip leadership, and supporters say a leaderless Nvidia weakens American AI capacity. Trump wrote that the country needs his leadership.
  • Backers separate his personal conduct from the national-security questions and point to government input the board received. Attorney General Todd Blanche called Nvidia a good corporate citizen.
  • Industry voices argue that tight export limits push China toward its own supply. Huang has said before that restrictions have not slowed Chinese AI progress.
  • A White House spokesman has said licensed exports to designated end users after national security inspections differ from smuggled chips.

Positions commonly voiced by the White House, Nvidia's board and AI-industry supporters.

Oversight view
  • Lawmakers in both parties say reinstatement does not answer questions about diverted chips or Huang's relationship with a suspected Chinese spy. A House spokesperson said it changes nothing.
  • Democrats including Schumer, Warren and Wyden say presidential involvement in a private board decision raises national-security concerns. Wyden said national security is for sale.
  • Republican China hawks such as Cotton want statutory limits. Cotton said Congress will pass bipartisan chip export-control bills.
  • Gallego and others want scrutiny of stock trades made before the announcement. Blanche and FBI Director Patel avoided questions on the Justice Department's role.

Positions commonly voiced by members of both parties, including Schumer, Warren and Cotton.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Nvidia's board announced on Monday, Nov. 23 that Huang is again president and CEO after resigning earlier in the month. The board said it had input from senior levels of the U.S. government (NBC News, CNBC).
  • Trump posted that the board made the correct and proper decision to reinstate Huang, and said he spoke directly with its members (MS NOW, Washington Post).
  • Huang is scheduled for transcribed House committee interviews on Dec. 1 and 2 and a public Senate Banking Committee hearing on Dec. 3 (NBC News, CBS News).
  • Nvidia shares fell about 6% on Tuesday after Monday's rally (Yahoo Finance). Blanche largely avoided questions on Justice Department actions related to Huang at a Tuesday news conference (NBC News).

Sourced from NBC News, CBS News, CNBC and PBS NewsHour (AP), November 25, 2026.

  • Real incomes are under pressure. Inflation-adjusted after-tax income fell 0.3% in September.
  • AI-linked stocks remain fragile, with Nvidia's comeback reversing Tuesday, and the Fed is expected to keep raising rates.

Positions commonly voiced by inflation-focused economists and market bears.

3. Administration readies new tariffs on China, EU and 14 other economies

WSJ and AP reports citing people familiar say USTR Jamieson Greer could release the excess-capacity findings soon; no final rates were public in the reports reviewed.

CONSERVATIVE
LIBERAL
BOTH SIDES
Conservative view
  • Government-backed overcapacity lets foreign producers sell underpriced goods abroad and undercut U.S. manufacturers, Greer argues.
  • China's exports and surplus show the scale of the problem. Its trade surplus reached a record near $1.2 trillion last year, and its overcapacity spans autos, solar, cement and steel.
  • The plan includes possible triggers for higher rates if countries do not curb overcapacity. Supporters see that as leverage for policy concessions.
  • Using a Section 301 investigation, rather than the emergency powers the Supreme Court rejected, answers the court's ruling by following a defined legal process.

Positions commonly voiced by the Trump administration, USTR Greer and tariff supporters. Some free-trade conservatives disagree.

Liberal view
  • Tariffs raise costs for households. The Footwear Distributors and Retailers of America said new 301 duties would raise costs for American families.
  • The list reaches close allies, including Japan, South Korea and all 27 EU members, which critics say risks trade conflict.
  • Exporters warn of retaliation. The American Soybean Association cautioned about Chinese retaliation.
  • Congressional Democrats plan to reclaim tariff authority and use oversight to test the administration's case, noting that the Constitution gives taxing power to Congress.

Positions commonly voiced by congressional Democrats, retail groups and farm-export advocates.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • AP, citing two people familiar with the matter, reported the administration is expected to soon announce higher tariffs on China, the EU and 14 other economies. The WSJ reported Greer could unveil findings as soon as this week.
  • The Supreme Court struck down tariffs imposed under IEEPA in February 2026 (The Hill). AP described the new move as following that ruling.
  • Per the WSJ, via Investing.com, the duties would cover most products from the targeted countries and exclude steel, aluminum and autos already under national-security tariffs. China would face the steepest rates.
  • Industry comments reported by Ecommerce Paradise included the retailers' and soybean growers' objections. Those objections are reported secondhand, and the source cites Reuters for a separate hearing remark.

Sourced from AP via myMotherLode, Investing.com, The Hill and Ecommerce Paradise, November 25, 2026.

SHUTDOWN WATCH

4. Dec. 11 funding deadline splits Democrats over using a stopgap to curb the Iran war

The White House sent Congress a stopgap wish list assuming a March 31 extension, while Democrats debate whether to risk a shutdown.

CONSERVATIVE
LIBERAL
BOTH SIDES
Conservative view
  • A stopgap should carry no war restrictions. Senate Majority Leader John Thune called Van Hollen's demand short-sighted and said Democrats are risking a shutdown.
  • Government and defense programs need continuity. The White House asked for billions for Golden Dome, Navy submarines and Artemis, and said more Pentagon requests are expected amid the war.
  • Cutting offensive funding mid-conflict has strategic costs. Democratic Sen. Mark Kelly said he does not want to leave Iran in control of the Strait of Hormuz.
  • A shutdown may not end the fighting, and any war powers resolution faces a Trump veto. Some House Democrats make the first point themselves.

Positions commonly voiced by Senate Majority Leader Thune, Republican appropriators and the White House.

Liberal view
  • Van Hollen, Duckworth and Gillibrand want any stopgap to bar funds for offensive operations in Iran. Khanna and Ansari said they would vote against a package that does not.
  • After a midterm landslide, the funding deadline is the party's first leverage point on the war, and base pressure to use it is intense.
  • A pragmatic wing, including senior House Democrats, favors a short-term extension to avoid a shutdown and a fuller fight once Democrats control Congress.
  • Schumer has urged an end to the war without specifying a plan. Others suggest narrower limits, such as barring ground troops or limiting transfers to the Pentagon.

Positions commonly voiced by Democrats including Van Hollen, Khanna and, on the cautious side, DeLauro.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Lawmakers face a Dec. 11 deadline to extend funding for most federal agencies (CBS News).
  • The White House sent Congress an anomalies list assuming a stopgap through March 31 (Punchbowl News). It asks for billions for Golden Dome, Navy submarines and Artemis (The Hill).
  • House Democrats are divided over whether to demand an end to war funding or defer the fight to next year, when they control Congress (The Hill).
  • Rosa DeLauro, who is set to chair House Appropriations, told CBS before the election she would support cutting off funds for the Iran war. Other top House Democrats are backing away from that approach (CBS News).

Sourced from CBS News, Punchbowl News, The Hill and MS NOW, November 25, 2026.

AI RULES

5. Governors urge Congress to keep state AI powers as preemption push continues

Bipartisan National Governors Association principles issued Nov. 22 seek federal oversight of frontier models while states keep authority over consumer and worker protections.

INNOVATION
OVERSIGHT
INNOVATION vs OVERSIGHT
Innovation view
  • A single national rulebook avoids a patchwork. The White House argues that regulating AI development raises interstate commerce and national security concerns.
  • The Great American AI Act discussion draft would preempt state AI laws for three years and bar state laws targeting model development.
  • The draft leaves states free to regulate how AI is used, which supporters present as a division of labor that protects competitiveness.
  • Trump backed a Republican push to add preemption language to the defense bill, according to Bloomberg via Startup Fortune.

Positions commonly voiced by the White House, House sponsors of federal AI framework drafts and industry advocates.

Oversight view
  • The governors' council, led by Democrats Wes Moore and Josh Stein and Republicans Mike Braun and Spencer Cox, calls for independent audits of frontier models and reporting of serious incidents.
  • States should keep authority to protect consumers, workers and children where AI is deployed.
  • Congress should approach preemption cautiously, displacing state law only as needed for a clearly stated national objective.
  • State action is already under way. New York's governor signed a five-year ban on AI-chatbot toys on Tuesday.

Positions commonly voiced by governors of both parties, state officials and AI-safety advocates.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Townhall, citing Nextgov, reported the Great American AI Act draft would allow federal preemption of state AI laws for three years.
  • Startup Fortune reported no bill text combining the governors' division of labor with White House preemption language has been published.
  • The NGA released the principles on Sunday, Nov. 22, to inform the incoming Congress (PYMNTS). The council proposes that Washington oversee national-scale risks and states govern how AI is used.
  • Gov. Kathy Hochul signed the AI toy ban Tuesday. It takes effect in February, making New York the second state after California to ban such toys (NBC News).

Sourced from PYMNTS, Startup Fortune, Townhall and NBC News, November 25, 2026.

6. NFL's first Thanksgiving Eve game puts Packers-Rams on Netflix

Both teams are 6-4, Micah Parsons is expected to return, and U2 headlines halftime, while fans are split over the streaming-only format.

TAKE
COUNTER-TAKE
TAKE vs COUNTER-TAKE
Take
  • Fans want it. In a survey cited by Yahoo Sports, 66.1% plan to watch and 65.7% support making Thanksgiving Eve an annual tradition.
  • The game has real stakes. Both teams are 6-4 and in the NFC playoff race, and Parsons is expected to return from last year's season-ending injury.
  • Netflix and the NFL are building an event, with U2 at halftime aimed at reaching beyond core football fans.
  • The business case is strong. RotoWire projects $131.7 million in sportsbook wagers, about 47% above the regular-season average.

Positions commonly voiced by the NFL, Netflix and fans welcoming more holiday football.

Counter-take
  • Streaming-only access draws objections. In the same survey, 45.3% oppose the game airing on Netflix rather than cable or broadcast.
  • Sen. Tammy Baldwin said the exclusive Netflix airing makes Wisconsin families pay for another service. Fans outside the local market need Netflix or NFL+.
  • The holiday week keeps expanding. A Wednesday game now joins the Thursday tripleheader and a Black Friday game.
  • Roger Goodell said two years ago he was not a fan of Wednesday games, citing the short recovery time for teams. Both teams here are coming off byes.

Positions commonly voiced by fans frustrated by streaming costs, Sen. Baldwin and critics of schedule expansion.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Packers at Rams is set for SoFi Stadium on Wednesday, Nov. 25, exclusively on Netflix. CBS Sports lists an 8 p.m. ET kickoff, while Yahoo Sports lists 8:15 p.m. ET.
  • CBS Sports said Parsons is expected to make his return Wednesday night after last year's season-ending injury.
  • The Yahoo Sports survey reported 66.1% plan to watch, 65.7% back an annual game and 45.3% oppose Netflix-only airing. RotoWire projects $131.7 million in wagers versus an $89.5 million average.
  • Fox News reported Baldwin criticized the Netflix-only airing and promoted her For the Fans Act. AOL reported Goodell's earlier comments on Wednesday games.

Sourced from Yahoo Sports, Fox News, CBS Sports and AOL, November 25, 2026.
