WHITE HOUSE SHAKEUP

1. Trump Names Stephen Miller Chief of Staff, Replacing Susie Wiles

The change comes days after Republicans lost control of Congress in the midterms.

CONSERVATIVE
LIBERAL
BOTH SIDES
Conservative view
  • Miller has worked for Trump since 2015 and is trusted to carry out the president's agenda without dilution.
  • A Democratic Congress will open investigations, and a combative operator suits that fight. One Trump adviser called Miller a "wartime adviser."
  • Immigration, tariffs and spending can be advanced by executive action where Congress is gridlocked, and Miller has helped identify such options.
  • Wiles stays in Trump's political operation, so the move reads as a role change rather than a purge.

Positions commonly voiced by Trump allies and conservative commentators, including Trump advisers and Fox News analysts.

Liberal view
  • The pick shows little has been learned from the midterm losses, according to several congressional Democrats.
  • Miller is the architect of the administration's most controversial immigration policies, and elevating him doubles down on them.
  • Governing by executive order sidesteps the new Democratic majority and the oversight voters just chose.
  • Even some Republicans object. Sen. Thom Tillis called Miller "the worst of all possible choices."

Positions commonly voiced by congressional Democrats and other critics, including GOP Sen. Thom Tillis.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • Trump announced the change on Truth Social on Nov. 6. Miller, currently deputy chief of staff, starts Monday, Nov. 9, and Wiles moves to the Trump Political Operation (Axios, The Hill).
  • A White House official said Wiles resigned Thursday night (The Hill). Axios reported she had offered to quit for two days before that.
  • AP counts show Democrats holding 54 Senate seats to 44 for Republicans and 254 House seats to 162, with 19 House races uncalled (Yahoo News midterms live blog).
  • Fox News reports Miller and Russ Vought have been identifying executive actions on immigration, tariffs and spending. The report says "reportedly" and was not independently confirmed in other outlets reviewed.

Sourced from Axios, The Hill, ABC News and Fox News, November 07, 2026.

AI & THE MIDTERMS

2. AI Stocks Rebound After Midterm-Driven Selloff Pushed Nasdaq Into Correction

Investors are weighing whether a Democratic Congress will tighten AI and data center rules.

INNOVATION
OVERSIGHT
INNOVATION vs OVERSIGHT
Innovation view
  • Trillions in lost market value show how much US growth now rests on AI investment, and new restrictions put that at risk.
  • AI-related construction and manufacturing jobs appear in the October jobs data, so curbs on data centers would cost real jobs.
  • A patchwork of state data center conditions would slow the build-out and favor competitors abroad.
  • Rules should come from one national framework rather than a rush of new restrictions, a stance the administration has taken.

Positions commonly voiced by AI industry executives, investors and Trump administration officials.

Oversight view
  • Voters in many communities backed Democrats who vowed to push back on data centers over electricity costs and water use.
  • Warnings that AI could escape human control justify enforceable safety standards and testing, as in Sen. Maria Cantwell's framework.
  • Investors already worried about a bubble, so the selloff may reflect stretched valuations more than regulation.
  • Industry benefits should be broadly shared, and rules can protect residents while still allowing responsible investment.

Positions commonly voiced by Democratic lawmakers such as Sen. Maria Cantwell, data center opponents and AI safety advocates.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • On Thursday, Nov. 5, the Nasdaq fell 5.52% to 24,315.00, a correction of more than 10% from its October record. The Dow lost 1,833.06 points and the S&P 500 fell 4.36% (CNBC).
  • On Friday, Nov. 6, the Nasdaq rose 1.1%, the S&P 500 rose 0.3% and the Dow fell 0.46%. All three posted weekly losses, and Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's KOSPI each fell about 6% (Reuters via Yahoo Finance).
  • Capital Economics' Joe Maher said the trigger appeared to be concern that Democrats in Congress and the states would regulate AI and restrict data centers (CBS News). NPR put the rout at about $2 trillion.
  • Cantwell is set to retake the Senate Commerce chair when the new term begins (PC World). Several lawmakers have urged Democrats to pass AI safety and data center bills once they take over in January (NBC affiliate report).

Sourced from CNBC, Reuters via Yahoo Finance, CBS News and NPR, November 07, 2026.

3. US Adds 165,000 Jobs in October as Unemployment Falls to 4.1%

Hiring beat forecasts, but wages trail inflation and long-term joblessness is up.

BULL
BEAR
BULL vs BEAR
Bull view
  • Payrolls of 165,000 were far above forecasts, a strong rebuttal to fears of a meaningful slowdown, per Principal's Seema Shah.
  • The labor force grew by 421,000 after shrinking, and the household survey showed 463,000 more people working.
  • Manufacturing added 19,000 jobs, and the sector has added more than 100,000 since its December 2025 low.
  • Revisions were positive and unemployment fell, which supports a resilient consumer.

Positions commonly voiced by market strategists, White House officials and growth-oriented economists.

Bear view
  • Average hourly earnings rose 3.1% from a year earlier, below inflation. Heather Long noted inflation has erased wage gains for seven straight months.
  • Long-term unemployed workers made up 27.6% of the jobless, and their rising share can keep jobless claims low while hiding distress.
  • Low turnover signals a stagnant market, per KPMG's Diane Swonk, and some gains came from AI-related construction.
  • A strong report strengthens the case for a Fed rate hike in December, which could slow the economy.

Positions commonly voiced by labor economists and inflation-focused analysts.

SOURCED
VERIFIED
WHAT'S DOCUMENTED
  • The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported 165,000 jobs added in October. Forecasts differed: 59,000 (Dow Jones consensus, CNBC) and 65,000 (FactSet, CBS News).
  • The unemployment rate fell to 4.1% from 4.2%. Manufacturing added 19,000 jobs and private payrolls rose 159,000 (Fox Business).
  • Wages grew 3.1% year over year (NBC News). ABC15 cited expected inflation of 3.7%.
  • Long-term unemployed workers were 27.6% of all unemployed (CBS News, CNBC). CBS reported economists see the data supporting a December rate hike. Morningstar had already described a quarter-point hike as widely expected.

Sourced from CNBC, CBS News, NBC News and Fox Business, November 07, 2026.
